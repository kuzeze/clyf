The problem

Climbers guess
how long to rest.

The pump

On a hard route the forearms give out first. Grip fades, and the climb ends.

The shake-out

Hanging off a jug mid-route, you rest with no idea when you have actually recovered.

The cost

Rest too little and you fall off the next move. Rest too long and you burn the session.

Maps give drivers an ETA. Climbers get guesswork.

CLYF02
Our solution

A forearm battery
with an ETA.

GRIPPING

Effort above a sustainable level drains the battery.

RESTING

It recharges, with a countdown: “Ready in 12 s”.

RELAX FULLY

EMG sees a tense forearm and slows the recharge.

READY

At 80% it glows and chimes: climb on.

MyoWare 2.0 + cable shield

Arduino UNO Q

What to trust

Measured, then modelled.

Measured

  • Muscle effort, as % of your own max grip
  • Hand on the hold
  • How long each grip and rest lasted
  • How relaxed the forearm is while resting

Estimated

  • Battery level and “ready in” countdown, from a W′-balance style model
  • Labelled as an estimate on screen
  • Demo constants, not yet fitted per climber
Spiky EMGCalibrating from peaks made hard grips read 30–40%. Per-second averages fixed it.
Loose electrodesA pinned or flat signal is flagged, not turned into a number.
Campus Wi-FiDevice isolation blocked the link, so data runs over USB.
No resistorThe FSR floats without one, so it works as a contact switch for now.
